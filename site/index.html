CLATTER POCKET

A generative drum machine hiding inside your FM-1.

Seven voices of authentic LXR drum synthesis. Generative kits, evolving rhythms, live recording and melodic percussion, running directly on the M-VAVE FM-1.

01 What it does

Authentic LXR synthesis

Synthesis running on the device, not sample playback. The LXR-02's open-source sound engine runs on the FM-1, compiled from the firmware's own DSP files. Seven drum tracks: DRUM 1, 2 and 3, SNARE, CYMBAL, CLOSED HAT and OPEN HAT. They are the LXR's seven triggers on its six voices, because the two hats share one.

Host tests match CLATTER's engine to within 3 steps of 16-bit audio.

Generative kits

Generate a complete kit, or a new sound for one instrument and leave the rest alone.

INIT and 16 factory kits, each a character the generator leans towards:

CLASSIC ANALOG DIGITAL INDUSTRIAL MINIMAL GLITCH TECHNO ELECTRO DUB NOISE METALLIC SOFT BROKEN ACID LO-FI CHAOS

Generative rhythms

Generate patterns in CLATTER's 11 rhythmic styles, regenerate one track, or mutate what is already there so it stays related.

MACHINE ELECTRO BREAKS JUNGLE HALFTIME BROKEN GARAGE INDUSTRIAL GLITCH POLYMETER DUB

The keyboard is the sequencer

Press SEQ and the 16 white keys are a page of the selected track's steps, lit where a step is on. Tap to add or remove, as on a classic x0x. The first four black keys are the pages. Hold SEQ and turn KNOB 3 for more of them.

Copy a page with SEQ+OCT−, go to another and paste it with SEQ+OCT+: every track and everything on its steps, as one undo.

Hold a step and turn a knob for its velocity, pitch, condition or variation. Tap and hold are told apart, so a hold never toggles.

The key lamps are on or off, so unlit steps are dark rather than dim.

Two keyboard modes

DRUM: three banks of the same nine sounds, each a variation of the last.

PITCH: the selected instrument chromatically. The patch does not change, so it returns exactly.

Live recording

Record drum performances and pitched percussion straight into the sequencer. Playing, hits overdub onto the nearest step. Stopped, enter and remove steps one at a time.

Up to 64 steps on four pages, seven tracks, a note or a variation and a condition on each step. One undo and redo for everything.

Saved on the device

9 projects in user Flash, surviving a restart. A project is the kit and the pattern together.

A save is checked before it counts, and writes only to sectors found blank or already its own.

MIDI input

Note-ons play and record like the keys, at their own velocity, in DRUM a different sound on every note from C1, in the order of the screen, or General MIDI drum notes.

Input over DIN was run in the emulator; USB and Bluetooth have not been exercised. MIDI clock in is followed, with start, stop and continue. There is no MIDI output and no thru.

02 The panel

The buttons do the same thing on every page, and the knobs follow the page. Pick a control to see what it does, then hold SAVE to see its second action. SELECT is an encoder with no push switch, so SAVE is the modifier.

All controls as a table

From POCKET-CONTROLS.md and POCKET-STORAGE.md. Encoder positions come from the emulator, not a device.

04 Quick start

In brackets, the key for the same control in the demo.

  1. Select a kit. Turn PRESETS ↑ ↓. INIT comes first, then the 16 factory kits.
  2. Generate a rhythm. Turn ALGORITHM , . to choose a style, then press ARP 9.
  3. Start playback. Press PLAY Space. Everything below works while it plays.
  4. Generate new sounds. Press ENV 3 for a kit. Hold SAVE 8 and press ENV for the selected voice only.
  5. Switch keyboard modes. Press SCL 2: KEYS: DRUM becomes KEYS: PITCH. OCT− Z and OCT+ X then change the octave.
  6. Program steps on the keys. Press SEQ 0. Tap a white key to turn a step on or off, tap one of the first four black keys for a page, and turn SELECT ← → for another track. Hold a step and turn a knob to edit it.
  7. Record a pattern. Press REC R. Playing, what you hit lands on the nearest step. Stopped, it lands on the selected step, and hitting it again removes it. On SEQ, REC gives the keys back to play.
  8. Take it back. Hold SAVE and press OCT− 8+Z to undo, kit or pattern. OCT+ 8+X redoes it.
  9. Save a project. Stop first. Tap SAVE 8, choose a slot with SELECT, then tap SAVE again. On a used slot, tap again while it says OVERWRITE. OCT+ loads, OCT− erases, and asks first. A project keeps the kit, the pattern, the tempo and the swing.

The full guide is POCKET-CONTROLS.md.

How it works

The FM-1 updates over USB-MIDI SysEx, not WebUSB. The installer runs M-VAVE's own update code, unchanged, in Chrome or Edge on a computer:

  1. Connect the FM-1 by USB, and allow MIDI with SysEx.
  2. The page checks the device and the package.
  3. Review the version, the checksum and the warning, and confirm.
  4. Install, and keep it connected.
  5. It finishes when the FM-1 restarts and reports this exact build.

What it needs

  • An FM-1 running M-VAVE's stock V15 or official Open firmware. From stock, the installer first installs official Open, M-VAVE's own package, then asks before going on. It will not go over any other firmware.
  • Your projects, samples and voices backed up first with M-VAVE's workbench, on official Open. That backup is not available on stock V15, or while CLATTER Pocket is installed.
  • A USB data cable, with other MIDI software closed.

FIRST-HARDWARE-TEST.md is the procedure for the first installation.

06 Recovery

None of these routes has been tried with CLATTER Pocket on a device.

  • Recovery procedure: restoring official firmware, and when to stop.
  • SAFE MODE: hold HOME 1.5 s after power-on. The engine stays off and the updater keeps running.
  • Update, recovery and backup: what is known, and what is not.
  • M-VAVE's official workbench: the restore route.
  • The FM-1 Firmware Hub's rescue guide: the community's tools, for other firmwares. Its OCT− USB rescue is not something CLATTER Pocket provides or has been tested with.

07 FAQ

What is CLATTER Pocket?

An independent, open-source firmware for the M-VAVE FM-1 that makes it a generative drum machine. It brings CLATTER's kit and rhythm generation and the LXR-02's sound engine to the device's own keys, buttons and screen.

Which hardware does it support?

The M-VAVE FM-1 only. M-VAVE does not document hardware revisions, so whether every unit behaves the same is not known.

Does it replace the original firmware?

Yes. It replaces the instrument and keeps the platform underneath: USB, Bluetooth, audio, the panel and the update protocol. The stock synth, its sequencer and effects are not available while it is installed, and neither is the workbench's backup of projects, samples and voices.

Can I restore the original firmware?

That is the design: the FM-1 reports itself as fm1_open, so M-VAVE's official workbench should be able to install official Open or V15 over it. It has not been done on a device. Read Recovery.

Does it use samples?

No sample playback. Every hit is synthesised live, and a kit is a set of parameters. The LXR engine does use wavetables and twelve short transient tables, as part of its synthesis.

Can I control it with MIDI?

Note input, yes: notes over DIN, USB and Bluetooth take the same path as the keys. Only DIN has been exercised, in the emulator. MIDI clock in is followed, with start, stop, continue and song position, confirmed over USB on one unit. There is no MIDI output and no thru.

Can I save patterns and kits?

Yes: 9 projects, each a kit and a pattern together, in the FM-1's user Flash. Tested on a device as well as in the emulator, including after a power-off. Back up anything you cannot afford to lose; there is no export. GLOBAL's playing settings survive a power-off too.

Can I try it without hardware?

Yes. The demo runs the firmware in the FM-1 emulator, in your browser.

Is installation currently safe?

It is tested on one FM-1. Firmware {{version}} was installed over other firmware and from stock V15, played, then left for official V15 and installed again, all on one unit (the report). Bluetooth, DIN MIDI, SAFE MODE and long runs are untested, and one unit cannot cover every FM-1. The installer keeps M-VAVE's compatibility and integrity checks. Back up first: moving between firmwares can erase projects and samples.

It is unproven. Firmware {{version}} has not run on a physical FM-1. The first version tried on one, 0.8.1, reset in a loop at the start-up screen until it was restored. 0.9.0 runs on that unit, with sound, USB audio, MIDI notes, MIDI clock over USB and saving. Bluetooth, long runs and leaving for official firmware are untested, so its safety is unknown. The installer keeps M-VAVE's compatibility and integrity checks, but those cannot show how the firmware behaves on a device. Install it only if you accept that a firmware that does not start could need recovery tools, or M-VAVE's help.

No, not yet. The firmware is not proven on a physical FM-1, so its safety is unknown, and this site will not install it. A firmware that does not start could need recovery tools, or M-VAVE's help.
